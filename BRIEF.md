## Concept as briefed
- Three patient personas who tell their story. Surgeon questions them and works out the recommended lens
- Polly's flow (her working file, slide 7): surgeon comes up, listens to the patient ("I'm XX old, I like XX, I work in XX, what would you recommend for me?"), an ORTHOPTIST provides the medical stats, surgeon selects a lens, headphones off, IOL Manager takes them to the lounge to discuss options. (The call transcript's "Optus" was Orthoptist.) Her patient is passive; ours makes the surgeon do the asking. That is the point of difference to show side by side in the deck
- The headphones-off handover to the lounge is the moment the whole thing is built for; argues for the consult summary the surgeon carries to the IOL Manager (Option B)
- TSA considered a hologram; expensive, needs filming. Jono has steered to avatars on screen
- Surgeon should draw out the details, not be handed them. Prompts natural; if key details are missed the patient volunteers them ("I forgot to mention, I drive a lot at night")

- **`orientation` exists only on `POST /v2/embeddings`**: enum `horizontal` (16:9) / `vertical` (9:16), default horizontal, added 22 Apr 2026. **But /v2/embeddings is a different product** — it returns a hosted URL and a cross-origin iframe. Going that way means losing the video element entirely, and with it clean capture mode, the transcript, the operator controls, the reveal card and the chroma-key route. Not a swap, a rebuild backwards
- Stock *studio* looks ship as separate landscape and portrait avatar_ids for the same look (e.g. Alessandra in Black Suit: landscape `9c59a215-…`, portrait `de82b2cc-…`). There is no field identifying orientation; only a table in the docs. **Custom video-trained avatars have no documented portrait variant at all**
- **THE ANSWER IS CSS, AND IT IS ALREADY BUILT.** `public/index.html` renders the stream full-bleed with `object-fit: cover`. On a 55" portrait screen a 1080p landscape stream fills the height and crops the sides, which centres and tightens on the face — the framing the design asks for anyway. The cost is the left and right of frame, which for a chest-up portrait is no loss. Set `LIVEAVATAR_VIDEO_QUALITY=very_high` (already set) so there are enough pixels to crop into
- Worth noting for the Tavus comparison: Tavus has the same gap — no orientation field on conversations — so portrait is a client-side crop there too

### 30 Oct 2026 — idle clips were never keyed. My bug, on every machine.

ALCON 2 showed Peter on studio green instead of Alcon blue. The diagnosis from that
machine was right and the cause is mine, not Windows, not Edge, not the Intel GPU.

**What was wrong.** `startChroma()` is called in one place, for the live stream only. The
`#idle` video element is never keyed, and the idle clips I cut on 29 Oct came straight off
the Tavus uploads, which are raw green. The Mac's clips are the same green - corner colour
#54824C against ALCON 2's #53804B, the difference being compression. **The Mac looked
right only because it was being watched live.** Any machine sitting idle shows green.

**Fix: the clips are now pre-keyed onto #003595** rather than keyed at runtime. Better for
a kiosk - the idle loop runs most of the day, and this way it costs no GPU at all, cannot
fail if WebGL misbehaves on a given machine, and flat blue compresses far better: Peter
went from 3.4MB of noisy green to 1.1MB.

**Trade-off to remember:** the clips now carry the key baked in. If the chroma settings
ever change, they must be re-exported. The command is below.

**Caught by looking rather than measuring.** My first export used ffmpeg
`chromakey=KEY:0.10:0.04` and the numbers looked fine - background came out #013594, which
is #003595 within H.264 rounding. But the rendered frames showed all three subjects as
near-transparent ghosts: at 0.10 the faces were going partly transparent and blending into
the blue, and a background-only measurement cannot see that. **This green is about 0.15
from skin, so the usable window is tiny** - the same lesson as the WebGL shader on 29 Oct,
learned twice. At 0.04/0.02 the skin reads #7A5251 against an unkeyed target of #7C5454,
and the subjects are clean.

Export command, per clip, with the key sampled from a 300x300 background patch rather than
one pixel so compression noise cannot pick it:

    ffmpeg -i raw-green-originals/<n>.mp4 \
      -filter_complex "color=c=0x003595:s=1920x1080[bg];\
      [0:v]chromakey=0x<KEY>:0.04:0.02[fg];[bg][fg]overlay=shortest=1,format=yuv420p" \
      -an -c:v libx264 -preset slow -crf 20 -movflags +faststart <n>.mp4

Originals kept at `public/idle/raw-green-originals/`. The USB stick is updated so the next
machine gets keyed clips built in.

  the two PCs that disagree. Getting this wrong would have logged the host out mid-consult.
- The message is plain: "The other room rejected the PIN. CONTROL_PIN must match on both PCs."
- `send()` on the phone now prefers the server's own message over a bare status code.

### 3 Nov 2026 — why the updater stopped on ALCON 1: Cloudflare rewrites our HTML

`UPDATE.ps1` refused the control page on ALCON 1 — expected `ed81b677...`, got `d4daf40e...` —
and changed nothing, which is the script behaving correctly. `server.js` matched, so the transfer
itself was sound.

**The file was never wrong.** The Mac's `public/control/index.html` is `ed81b677...` on disk and
over the tunnel, untouched since 30 Oct 15:25. The bytes change in flight: **Cloudflare injects
its Web Analytics beacon into anything it serves as HTML to a browser-looking request.** Proved it
rather than guessed — the same URL fetched with `Accept: */*` gives `ed81b677...`, and with a
browser User-Agent and `Accept: text/html` gives exactly `d4daf40e...`, ALCON 1's hash. The diff is
one line, 367 bytes, a `<script src="static.cloudflareinsights.com/beacon.min.js">` before
`</body>`. The tell was in the headers: the HTML response had **no `Content-Length` and no
`ETag`** while `server.js` had both — the signature of a response being rewritten as it streams.
My own check on Friday passed because curl does not ask like a browser.

**Fix: the control page is now served for transfer as `control-index.txt`, not `.html`.** Content
type `text/plain`, nothing rewrites it, `Content-Length` is back. `UPDATE.ps1` also now sends
`Accept: */*`. All four files verified byte-identical whether fetched plainly or as a browser —
that is the check that was missing the first time round.

**Lesson worth keeping: never hash-pin an HTML file fetched through Cloudflare.** The origin bytes
and the delivered bytes are not the same thing, and which you get depends on who is asking.

### 7 Nov 2026, 8am — Claude Code — post-mortem: why "both rooms on one page" died at the stand

Jono asked for this analysis during the show. It is a design error of mine from 29 Oct.

**What broke.** `/api/peer/room` returns 502 on both rooms, so each room's control page
shows the other room as offline. Each room itself is fine: both answer in under 0.2s, both
control pages serve, both are remotely tunable.

**Root cause, measured not guessed.** The failure returns in 0.19s. Timing distinguishes
the two candidates cleanly, tested against the current build on a spare port:

    PEER_URL = hostname that will not resolve   ->  0.188s, "fetch failed"
    PEER_URL = LAN IP that is not there         ->  4.015s, "aborted due to timeout"
    the live rooms                              ->  0.211s and 0.188s

So `PEER_URL` is a **hostname failing DNS**, not a stale LAN IP. My first diagnosis said
stale LAN IP and was wrong. Jono corrected it with "it worked yesterday", which was the
clue that mattered: DNS worked yesterday, and the venue only started hijacking port 53
today.

**Ruled out: UPDATE-7.** The only change to `server.js` since UPDATE-4 is 19 lines, all
inside the `/api/room/command` handler. Nothing touched `PEER_URL`, `peer()` or the
`/api/peer/*` routes. The peer code works: current build plus a valid address returns the
peer's state in 0.24s.

**The design error.** The 29 Oct entry above records the decision in my own words: relay
to the other room server side "so there is no cross-origin handling and either room's URL
gives you both rooms". The phone could already reach both rooms independently over two
tunnels. The relay replaced two independent paths with a chain, phone -> PC A ->
Cloudflare -> PC B, to avoid writing about three lines of CORS. The app has no CORS
handling and the control page has no absolute URLs, so the simpler design was never tried.

Three failure modes were created. Two have bitten:
1. 30 Oct: `CONTROL_PIN` must match on both PCs, because the relay authenticates with its
   own. Cost a debugging session, recorded above.
2. 7 Nov: the middle hop requires a *show PC* to resolve a public hostname. Venue DNS
   hijack killed it. The phone resolving correctly is irrelevant.
3. Untriggered: if one PC's app stops, its card disappears from the *other* room's page.
   The design claimed the opposite as its benefit.

**Why it is unrecoverable today.** `PEER_URL` is read once at boot. The fix needs a `.env`
edit or a hosts pin and a restart, both at a keyboard, and the PCs are sealed in the wall.
Same class of fault as the PAL cache being read once at boot, which has cost time three
times now.

**Proximate cause, also mine.** When I diagnosed the DNS hijack this morning I pinned one
hostname. `demo`, `room-a` and `room-b` are on the same zone and resolve to the same two
IPs (104.21.96.126, 172.67.180.194). One more line in the same command and the relay would
still be up. I fixed the symptom in front of me instead of the class of problem.

**The fix for the next build, not today.** Control page fetches each room by absolute URL;
`Access-Control-Allow-Origin` on the two read endpoints only. Two independent paths, no
shared PIN, no PC-to-PC dependency, and the phone does the resolving. Survives either PC
being down, which is what the relay was supposed to do. Also: pin all three hostnames in
`hosts` in the installer, and read `PEER_URL` per request instead of once at boot.

**Today's workaround, no PC access needed.** One bookmark per room, each host drives their
own:
    https://room-a.meetthepatient.com.au/control/
    https://room-b.meetthepatient.com.au/control/
Ignore the other-room card. Two bookmarks lose nothing, which is the tell: the relay added
three failure modes and no capability.

**Separate trap found while checking this.** `demo.meetthepatient.com.au/control/` serves a
control page off the **Mac in Melbourne**. It reports `roomId: A`, `displayMode: test`,
`hasPeer: false`, so it draws one card labelled "Room 1" and looks entirely normal while
driving nothing at the stand and spending Tavus minutes. It must not be on any host's
phone. Worth gating or relabelling in the next build.
