## ACT 1 · THE BRIEF

H2: The brief was patient first.

Get surgeons to take a history before they choose a lens, then hand them to the IOL Manager with
something to talk about.

So the patients were written to hold back. Each one opens with a greeting and nothing more. Their
daily life, what they want from surgery and what they would trade away only come out when the
surgeon asks for them. A surgeon who skips the history still has to make a recommendation, and the
patient tells them, politely, when it does not fit the life they have just described.

## ACT 3 · THE ROOMS

H2: Two private rooms, run from a phone.

Each room had a 55-inch portrait screen with the patient at seated eye level, a headset with a boom
mic and a chair. The IOL Manager chose the patient and started each consult from a phone, so nobody
on the stand touched the equipment in the wall. Between consults the screen held a waiting-room loop.

Every patient was filmed on green and shown on Alcon blue, so the rooms carry the brand without a
set build.
(Keep the green-to-#003595 swatch. Swatch captions: "Filmed on green" / "Shown on Alcon blue".)

Both rooms were monitored for the three days, and adjustments during show hours were made remotely,
without opening the stand.

## ACT 4 · NOTHING RECORDED

H2: Nothing a surgeon said was recorded.

H3: What the system keeps
The room, the patient chosen, the start time and the length of each consult. This report is built
from that and nothing else.

H3: What it never had
No transcript, no audio, no video and no names. Each conversation is deleted from the platform as
soon as the consult ends.

That matters when a surgeon is speaking to a stranger at a trade stand. It also means everything qualitative
in this report comes from the people who were in the room.

## ACT 5 · THE NUMBERS   (keeps the interactive chart)

H2: Every consult on the stand, end to end.

Each column is one consult. The dashed line is the time limit: seven minutes on day one, raised to
ten for days two and three because consults kept running into it.

H3: What the numbers show
42 consults ran a minute or longer, with a median of five minutes 38 seconds. 13 ran until the time
limit ended them, the longest at ten minutes seven seconds on the Sunday.

H3: What is counted
54 sessions were logged across the three days. The 12 under a minute were warm-ups and resets between
surgeons. They are shown in outline and left out of the median.
